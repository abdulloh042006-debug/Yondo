## 1. What does the Uzbek personal-data law (ZRU-547) require about WHERE data is stored, and what changed in 2024-2026?

### Takeaway
The strict "all Uzbek citizens' personal data must sit on servers in Uzbekistan" rule (Art. 27-1, in force from April 2021) was substantially relaxed by Law ZRU-1125 of 26 March 2026: only biometric data, genetic data and data of users of telecom operators' services must now be stored in Uzbekistan; everything else may be stored or processed abroad if at least one of three conditions is met (adequate country, approved standard contractual clauses/binding corporate rules, or approved international standards). A country list of 49 jurisdictions was adopted on 29 July 2026 (reported to include the US, UK, Japan, Germany, Singapore). Foreign clouds are therefore no longer automatically non-compliant, but the details (what counts as "telecom user data", what counts as "biometric", implementing acts) need a lawyer.

### Cited Findings
- Law "On Personal Data" is No. ZRU-547 of 2 July 2019, in force 1 October 2019; the lex.uz page lists later amending laws, including ZRU-880 (28 Nov 2023, Art. 34) and ZRU-1125 (26 Mar 2026, which amended Art. 3 part 2, Art. 20 part 1 and Art. 27-1) — [lex.uz, Law ZRU-547 (RU, unofficial translation)](https://lex.uz/docs/4396428)
- The same lex.uz page also lists a Constitutional Law ZRU-1158 (13 Jul 2026, adds part 3 to Art. 3 on territories with a special legal regime, effective 25 Jul 2026) and a law ZRU-1174 (11 Sep 2026) that amended Art. 25; the page warns the Art. 25 translation is still in progress. I could not verify what ZRU-1174 changed — [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396428)
- Art. 27-1 (current text): part 2 lists data subject to mandatory storage on the territory of Uzbekistan: biometric data of individuals; genetic data of individuals; data of users of services of telecommunications operators operating in Uzbekistan. Part 3: other personal data may be stored and processed outside Uzbekistan if one of three conditions holds: (1) the foreign state is recognised as providing adequate protection; (2) the operator adopts and follows standard contractual clauses or binding corporate rules meeting requirements set by the authorized body; (3) the operator follows international data-management/storage standards from a list approved by the authorized body. Part 4: the Cabinet of Ministers sets the list of adequate countries — [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396428)
- Art. 27-1 contains no separate exception for foreign-based operators; the part-3 conditions apply to any operator storing non-mandatory data abroad — [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396428)
- Art. 20 (registry): registration of a personal-data database is "by notification" (заявочный принцип); databases that must be stored in Uzbekistan under Art. 27-1 part 2 must be registered in the State Register; some categories are exempt (e.g. data made public by the subject, data limited to full names, non-automated processing); owners/operators must notify the authorized body of changes to registration data within ten calendar days — [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396428)
- Art. 8: the authorized state body is the State Personalization Center under the Cabinet of Ministers (per the lex.uz page). Art. 15: cross-border transfer is allowed to countries with adequate protection; to others only in limited cases (including the data subject's consent and international treaties); transfer can be banned or restricted to protect constitutional order, morals, health, rights, defence and security — [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396428)
- Art. 3: the law does not cover processing by an individual for personal/household purposes, archives, or state-secret data — [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396428)
- The March 2026 amendments were signed by the President on 26 March 2026 (Ministry of Justice press service); they take effect on official publication; the stated purpose is to give a legal basis for international payment systems (PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay) — [Anhor](https://anhor.uz/news/v-uzbekistane-88/); [Spot.uz, 27 Mar 2026](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/03/27/personal-data/)
- Legislative Chamber passed the amendments in January 2026 and the Senate in February 2026; before the change, Art. 27-1 (introduced 2021) required personal data to be stored on technical equipment physically located in Uzbekistan — [Spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/03/27/personal-data/)
- Original localization rule entered into force on 16 April 2021 (collect, systematise and store on technical means physically in Uzbekistan, with registration in the State Register of Personal Databases) — [DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/uzbekistan-data-localisation-rule-enters-force); [Legal500 / Vakhidov & Partners](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/personal-data-compliance-in-uzbekistan/)
- Adequacy list: a resolution adopted 29 July 2026 (published on lex.uz per the article) set a list of 49 countries and territories, naming about 20 incl. the US, UK, Japan, Germany, Singapore plus some British Crown Dependencies; the article did not give the full list, the resolution number or its effective date. Personal and anonymised data may move automatically through information systems created under international agreements to listed countries without notifying the authorized body; operators must prevent leaks; for a breach during cross-border transfer the operator must notify the competent authority within 24 hours and file a follow-up within 72 hours; transfers to unlisted countries need legal, organisational and technical safeguards; standard contractual clauses and corporate rules are approved by the Ministry of Internal Affairs, Ministry of Digital Technologies, State Security Service and the National Agency for Prospective Projects; the Ministry of Internal Affairs' Department for Migration and Personalization proposes list changes — [Kun.uz, 4 Aug 2026](https://kun.uz/en/news/2026/08/04/uzbekistan-approves-49-countries-for-simplified-personal-data-transfers)
- Further confirmation of the 26 Mar 2026 law number and content from a legal-information site (search summary, not fetched in full) — [advice.uz](https://advice.uz/ru/document/4613)
- Penalties (as of the 2021 tightening, effective 30 Jan 2022): Art. 46-2 of the Code of Administrative Liability covers illegal handling of personal data and failing to keep databases on technical means located in Uzbekistan; first-offence fine 7 BRV for individuals and 50 BRV for officials; repeat within a year falls under Criminal Code Art. 141-2 (fine up to 150 BRV / rights deprivation up to 3 years / corrective labour up to 2 years per GRATA-type reports; Buxgalter.uz puts the fine at 100-150 BRV, and 150-200 BRV for aggravated cases) — [GRATA](https://gratanet.com/news/in-uzbekistan-liability-for-the-breach-of-law-on-personal-data-has-been-tightened); [EY Uzbekistan](https://ey.com/en_uz/tax-alerts/2021/12/developments-in-the-field-of-protection-of-personal-data); [Kun.uz 2021](https://kun.uz/en/news/2021/10/31/uzbekistan-strengthens-liability-for-breaching-the-law-on-personal-data); [Buxgalter.uz](https://buxgalter.uz/ru/publish/doc/text174429_usilena_otvetstvennost_za_razglashenie_personalnyh_dannyh)
- Separate, later fine of 50-100 BRV (reported as up to ~41.2 million UZS) for illegal processing of personal data using artificial intelligence, with confiscation of the means — [Zamin.uz](https://zamin.uz/en/uzbekistan/183054-penalties-for-the-abuse-of-personal-information-through-artificial-intelligence-have-increased.html)
- Practical enforcement (2021): the telecom/informatization inspectorate (Uzkomnazorat) enforced localization by blocking Twitter, TikTok and VKontakte for almost the entire coverage period and briefly restricting Facebook, Telegram, YouTube and others in November 2021 — [Freedom House, Freedom on the Net 2022](https://freedomhouse.org/country/uzbekistan/freedom-net/2022). Kun.uz identified Art. 27-1 as the basis, and reported the head of Uzkomnazorat was fired for illegally blocking social networks — [Kun.uz, 4 Nov 2021](https://kun.uz/en/news/2021/11/04/head-of-uzkomnazorat-fired-for-illegally-blocking-social-networks-in-uzbekistan). By 2025, Twitter was unblocked (2022), TikTok remained inaccessible and the others were restored — [Daryo, 16 Jul 2025](https://daryo.uz/en/2025/07/16/uzbekistan-loses-up-to-45bn-annually-due-to-data-localization-law-report-finds/)
- Cost estimates of the old rule are advocacy/think-tank figures, not official: "up to $45bn annually" (Daryo headline, 2025) and "87% of foreign companies declined to enter" (Anhor, citing Kotib AI Research) — [Daryo](https://daryo.uz/en/2025/07/16/uzbekistan-loses-up-to-45bn-annually-due-to-data-localization-law-report-finds/); [Anhor](https://anhor.uz/news/law-7/)
- Local cloud providers market themselves as ZRU-547 compliant: UzCloud says all physical infrastructure is in Uzbekistan and the operator acts as a processor while the customer is the controller responsible for personal-data compliance (offer, sections 2.3 and 4.1) — [UzCloud public offer](https://uzcloud.uz/oferta); Pro-Data states compliance with "LRU No. 547" — [Pro-Data](https://www.pro-data.tech/en)

### Inferences
- Foreign clouds (Render, Neon, Hetzner, Oracle) are now arguably permissible for ordinary Yondo data (names, ages, city, bookings, chat, reports, photos) only if the chosen server region is in a country on the 29 July 2026 list, or Yondo adopts approved standard clauses, or meets a listed international standard. Which region you choose matters (US, UK, Germany and Singapore were named in the article; other countries such as Finland or the Netherlands were not confirmed either way in the text I could read). This is my reading of Art. 27-1 part 3 and the Kun.uz article, not an official statement.
- The "data of users of services of telecom operators" category is ambiguous. Read narrowly it means subscriber data held by telecom operators; read broadly it could catch any phone number of an Uzbek mobile subscriber that Yondo stores (the lex.uz wording and news paraphrases do not settle it). This is the single most important interpretation question for Yondo, because Yondo stores phone numbers and trusted-contact numbers. A lawyer/the authority must confirm.
- Face photos for a "verified companion" check may become biometric data if processed for identification (face matching, liveness). Plain profile photos are probably not, but the statutory definition of "biometric data" was not retrieved; if Yondo stores verification templates, those would have to be in Uzbekistan under the current text.
- Safest design for a launch with minimal legal risk: keep the primary production database and backups (at least anything with phone numbers, ID/verification artefacts, safety reports, trusted contacts) in an Uzbek data center; use foreign services only for non-personal workloads (static assets, CI, code). This avoids relying on the new adequacy list and on interpretation of the telecom-data category.
- Because Art. 20 was amended by ZRU-1125, whether a database that is entirely hosted abroad still has to be registered in the State Register is unclear to me; under the lex.uz wording the registration duty is tied to databases subject to mandatory storage in Uzbekistan. Unverified; lawyer to confirm.
- Since 2021 enforcement against foreign platforms was by blocking access (not fines on startups); for a small domestic startup the more realistic risks are administrative fines and a complaint-driven inspection. This is inference; I found no cases of fines against small startups.

### Gaps
- Not obtained: official lex.uz text of ZRU-1125 itself, the 29 July 2026 resolution (number, full 49-country list, effective date), the approved list of "international standards", and the approved standard-clause forms.
- Not confirmed: whether the State Personalization Center (named on lex.uz Art. 8) still exists as the authorized body or has been merged into the Ministry of Internal Affairs' migration and personalization department (Kun.uz Aug 2026 names the latter for list changes). Naming is inconsistent between sources.
- Not found: the current BRV (base calculation value) in UZS, so current fine amounts in UZS are unknown; the 2021 figure quoted (1,890,000 UZS for 7 BRV) is outdated. Not checked whether the March 2026 amendment also changed the administrative-liability article.
- Not found: any documented fine or inspection against a small Uzbek startup for localization or registration.
- Not read in full: Legal500 / MoFo / Dentons / GRATA briefs on the 2026 changes (links appeared in search but fetches failed or returned 404).

## 3. Is self-hosting on university lab PCs feasible and safe (closed beta), and what is the middle path?

### Takeaway
I could not complete research on this question (usage limit reached before searching it). What follows is limited to what the law and provider pages above imply plus clearly-labelled reasoning; no network-policy, ISP, CGNAT or university-policy sources were gathered.

### Cited Findings
- UzCloud's public offer treats the customer (not the hosting provider) as the controller responsible for personal-data compliance and the provider as a processor with no access to the application layer — this is the contractual model a university would also need to replicate if it hosted Yondo (a written processor-type agreement) — [UzCloud public offer](https://uzcloud.uz/oferta)
- Art. 20 puts duties on "owners or operators" of databases, including notifying the authorized body of changes in registration data within ten calendar days — [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/4396428)
- An Uzbek-hosted VM suitable for a closed beta costs roughly 256,000-320,000 UZS/month (see section 2), which is the realistic alternative to unmanaged lab hardware.

### Inferences
(All of these are my reasoning from general technical knowledge and the law above, not from fetched sources.)
- Legal: the university would physically hold data whose "operator" (the entity that decides purposes and means) is the Yondo legal entity; the safest structure is a written hosting/processing agreement with the university, plus written approval from the rector/IT governance, and a clear statement that Yondo, not the university, is responsible for the data. Using student-lab machines also risks mixing university liability with a commercial startup (conflict-of-interest / misuse-of-property questions for the owner as head of IT). A lawyer must check the university charter and the owner's employment contract.
- Data sensitivity: Yondo stores phone numbers, photos, chat, safety reports and trusted-contact numbers; a breach in a lab with student access is high-impact and could trigger the breach-notification duties described in section 1. First-year-student lab PCs usually lack UPS, ECC RAM, RAID, locked cabinets and access logging.
- Network: a typical Uzbek university or consumer-ISP link may be behind CGNAT or without a static public IP; a tunnel (e.g. Cloudflare Tunnel) removes the need for a public IP but routes all traffic through a foreign US company's edge, which a lawyer should assess under the cross-border rules. Not verified for any specific Uzbek ISP.
- Uptime: "not 100% guaranteed 24/7" means no SLA, power cuts and maintenance windows; acceptable only for a clearly-labelled closed beta with no real money, no real SOS function and a small, invited user group.
- Conditions I would suggest for lab PCs in a closed beta: synthetic/test data or a very small consenting tester group; full-disk encryption; locked room; UPS; automated encrypted off-site backups; no SOS or payment dependency; written university permission; no production use after the beta.
- Middle path (recommended direction): primary database and app on an Uzbek VPS (UzCloud, Ahost or similar; about 0.25-0.32 million UZS/month); encrypted backups to a second Uzbek location or provider (and only non-personal or properly-encrypted data abroad after legal sign-off); lab PCs used only for CI, staging with fake data, and replicas of non-personal assets.

### Gaps
- NOT RESEARCHED: Uzbek university IT/network policies, whether Uzbek ISPs/universities use CGNAT or sell static IPs, power-outage statistics, Cloudflare Tunnel terms for this use, any Uzbek regulation on hosting commercial services from state-institution networks, whether a state university can lawfully host a commercial third-party service.
- No source on the legal definition of "operator" vs "processor" in ZRU-547 was read (only Art. 20 wording).

## 6. Closing: plain summary for the owner and lawyer checklist

### Takeaway
The localization pressure eased in March 2026, but the safest and cheapest compliant path for launch is a small Uzbek VPS (about 0.26-0.32 million UZS/month) for the database and app, with registered legal entity first; lab PCs are only suitable for a tiny, clearly-labelled test with fake data.

### Plain 10-line summary for a non-lawyer owner (translate to Uzbek for the owner)
1. Until March 2026, the law said all Uzbek citizens' personal data had to be stored on servers inside Uzbekistan. Since the law of 26 March 2026 (ZRU-1125), only biometric data, genetic data and data of telecom users must stay in Uzbekistan.
2. Other data may now be stored abroad if the country is on the government list (49 countries, adopted 29 July 2026, reportedly including the US, UK, Germany, Japan, Singapore), or you use approved standard contract clauses, or you meet an approved international standard.
3. It is not yet clear whether phone numbers of Uzbek mobile users fall under the "telecom users" category; this must be checked by a lawyer because Yondo stores phone numbers.
4. Safest plan: keep the main database, phone numbers, safety reports and backups on a server inside Uzbekistan, and use foreign clouds only for non-personal things.
5. Uzbek VPS options that exist today: UzCloud/Uztelecom (about 256,000-284,000 UZS/month for a small server, open to individuals and sole proprietors), Ahost.uz (from 125,000 UZS/month; 320,000 for 2 cores/100 GB), plus Pro-Data and Uzinfocom for businesses (prices on request).
6. Lab PCs: no guaranteed power, internet or physical security, and a university mixing with a private business creates liability; use them only for testing with fake data and with the university's written permission.
7. Payme Business works only with registered legal entities (IP, LLC, etc.); you will need a registered legal entity and a bank account before real payment keys, and that same entity should be named as the data "operator" in the Terms and Privacy pages.
8. SMS: Eskiz reportedly charges 85 UZS per SMS (175 UZS for advertising to Ucell/Uzmobile) under a contract for legal entities; register the sender name early because Ucell blocks unregistered sender names since 3 August 2026.
9. Penalties found so far are modest for first offences (7 BRV individuals, 50 BRV officials, from the 2021 rules), but in 2021 the state actually blocked big platforms for breaking the localization rule.
10. Not yet researched: identity checks (MyID/OneID), licences for a companion marketplace and for holding customers' money, and Google Play/App Store rules; these need a second research pass and a lawyer.

### What to check with a lawyer
- Does "data of users of telecom operators' services" include ordinary users' phone numbers held by an app? What is the statutory definition of "biometric data" (do profile photos or verification selfies count)?
- Exact text and effective date of ZRU-1125, the 29 July 2026 adequacy-list resolution (number and full list; is the chosen cloud region's country on it), the approved standard clauses and the list of accepted international standards; what ZRU-1174 (11 Sep 2026) changed.
- Whether a database that is entirely stored abroad still needs to be notified to the State Register (Art. 20 as amended); who the authorized body is now (State Personalization Center vs the Ministry of Internal Affairs' department).
- Who is the "operator" of Yondo's data (the legal entity to be created), what legal form to use (IP/ChP vs LLC), and the data-processing agreement needed with any hosting provider, SMS gateway or payment provider.
- Current fine amounts in UZS (current BRV) and whether the March 2026 law changed the administrative/criminal provisions.
- Whether the university can lawfully host a commercial third-party service, and the head of IT's conflict-of-interest position.
- Whether routing traffic through a foreign proxy/tunnel (e.g. Cloudflare) counts as cross-border processing.
- Licensing: whether a paid-companionship marketplace needs any licence or registration; whether holding and forwarding customers' payments to companions requires a Central Bank licence or must be done through a payment provider's split-payment product; tax treatment of companions and the platform.
- Identity verification: whether MyID/OneID can be used by a private company, and what data may be stored.
- SOS/safety data: retention periods, access rights and breach-notification duties (24 h / 72 h for cross-border transfer breaches per Kun.uz).
- Consent texts and the age rules for under-18 users, and the data-subject rights procedures in the Terms/Privacy drafts.
